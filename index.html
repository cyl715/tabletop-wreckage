01Project overview

Reference game analysis: Splash Buster

Casual physics-destruction games have become a familiar mobile category: the player shoots at a stacked object, the stack collapses, and the payoff is the collapse itself. Splash Buster is the clearest demonstration of how well that loop works on a touch screen.

1.1Background of physics-destruction mobile games

In the reference game, the player adjusts the angle of a cannon and launches heavy balls at the weak points of stacked towers. Ammunition is limited, so progress depends on triggering chain reactions rather than on firing constantly. Tap-to-shoot controls keep the entry barrier low, and the title holds a 4.9 rating on the Play Store with more than one million installs — evidence that the framework travels well to a small screen.

1.2Limitations of the reference game, and the design motivation

The original loop is straightforward, but it is also thin. It offers one projectile type, and its levels are mostly randomly stacked clutter towers assembled with little regard for load-bearing structure. Players tend to clear stages by trial and luck, which leaves limited room for deliberate planning.

Building on the shooting-and-destruction framework, this project redefines the theme as a household tabletop. Instead of derelict buildings or clutter towers, levels sit on a flat coffee-table platform filled with stacked building bricks, drinking glasses and hard-cover books. A protected antique vase is introduced as the critical constraint, so the player's job is not destruction alone: it is destruction with a boundary.

02Project adaptation and design

From a demolition site to a coffee table

2.1A new theme, and a new objective

Keeping the core framework of the reference game, the project abandons the building-demolition theme and moves to a household coffee-table battlefield. Play revolves around knocking target household objects off the tabletop platform while the protected antique vase stays intact on the surface — a win condition that turns every shot into a decision about what might also move.

2.2Two-layer resource mechanism

Each level grants a fixed quota of 20 standard projectiles, which cannot be bought with coins. Coins are a persistent, cross-level currency earned after completing stages, and they serve two purposes: permanently unlocking cosmetic turret skins in the main-menu store, or buying one-off consumable special weapons during an active level. Either the heavy cannon or the machine gun can be bought mid-combat for a temporary advantage in that run.

Standard rounds

Per level · not purchasable · 20
  • The fixed budget that defines each level's difficulty.
  • Remaining rounds convert into stars at the end of a level.
  • Destroyed on impact with a target; they do not penetrate.

Coins

Persistent · cross-level · earned
  • Earned by completing stages, plus bonuses from stars.
  • Spent in the main-menu store on permanent cosmetic skins.
  • Spent inside a level on a one-off heavy cannon or machine gun.

2.3Four-direction turret shooting

The camera stays at a fixed 45-degree overhead angle, but the player may switch between four firing positions around the table — bottom, top, left and right. All four positions draw from the same ammunition pool, so switching is a change of angle rather than extra resources. This solves the ballistic dead-lock that a single firing direction creates when the protected vase stands between the cannon and a target.

2.4Level design and object physical rules

Scenes are re-imagined as household coffee-table platforms with three categories of interactive object. Each type behaves differently under fire, so the layout reads as a structure rather than a pile.

The vase rule

Mission failure is triggered only when a player-fired projectile directly collides with the vase. Flying debris from destroyed targets will not damage it. A projectile is destroyed when it hits a target object and cannot pass through, and a level is complete only when every target object is off the tabletop while the vase remains intact on the surface.

03Game fun and player challenge

What the player actually enjoys

The adaptation moves the pleasure of the game away from "knock it down" and toward "work out how it will come down" — with a fragile object in the room that punishes a careless answer.

3.1Achievement from observation and structural prediction

The first source of enjoyment is the satisfaction of reading a structure. Players examine the layout of stacked brick assemblies, identify which pieces carry load, and plan a firing direction by switching among the four turret positions. Hitting a critical supporting point and triggering a chain collapse of stacked household objects gives the strong, legible payoff that the reference game only delivers by accident.

3.2Strategic trade-offs and risk–reward decisions

The second is the pleasure of a genuine trade-off. Limited standard ammunition is the core challenge of each level, so the player has to decide whether to spend ordinary projectiles taking a structure down step by step, or to spend accumulated coins on a special weapon mid-battle for a burst of destructive power. Every one of those decisions also carries the risk of touching the protected vase. Several different strategies can complete a level, which is what makes replaying it worthwhile.

3.3A cathartic visual and audio destruction experience

The third is simply the release of it. Disintegrating glasses, tumbling books and collapsing brickwork, paired with matching impact sound, turn a correct structural decision into a physical event the player can watch happen.

04Core gameplay and systems

The loop, the rules, and the weapons

4.1Basic game flow and level rules

The project contains three designed household tabletop levels. On entering a level the player observes the tabletop scene through a fixed 45-degree overhead camera, switches among the four turret positions, and spends standard projectiles to shoot targets. The coffee-table surface is the judgement platform: a level is won when every target household object has been knocked off it and the protected vase remains intact, and lost when a player projectile directly hits the vase, or when all standard projectiles are gone while targets still stand on the table.

After completion, the player is awarded stars calculated from the remaining standard projectiles. More leftover ammunition means more stars, and stars add a coin bonus on top of the base reward for the stage — so the resource the level forces you to spend becomes the score you are measured on.

4.2Weapons and the in-level purchase mechanic

Two special consumable weapons exist. Neither is available by default: both require persistent cross-level coins, spent during level gameplay. The player can buy them several times within a single level as long as the coins last, and each purchase yields one use, consumed right after activation.

Heavy cannon

One high-impact projectile · coin purchase in-level
  • Effective against large brick load-bearing structures.
  • Answers the problem standard rounds cannot: a mass that has to be broken rather than nudged.
  • Single use per purchase.

Machine gun

Short manual burst · unlimited small projectiles
  • Best for clearing scattered fragile objects such as drinking glasses.
  • Trades precision for coverage within a short firing window.
  • Single use per purchase.

Standard projectiles

Supplied at a fixed quota of 20 per level and never purchasable with coins. A projectile is destroyed immediately on colliding with a target object and cannot penetrate it; debris from destroyed objects will not trigger damage to the antique vase.

05Project scope

What is in, what is optional, what is out

The scope is deliberately split three ways so that a solo developer protects the core: mandatory systems define the deliverable, bonus features are attempted only after those are complete, and out-of-scope content is named in advance rather than silently dropped later.

Mandatory core features

  • Household-themed level design
  • Physics for three object types: bricks, glasses, hard-cover books
  • Two weapon types plus the standard projectile
  • Two-layer resource system: fixed level ammunition, persistent cross-level coins
  • Collapse mechanics using kinematic rigid-body switching
  • Four-direction turret switching
  • Three complete playable levels
  • Basic UI, in-level special-weapon purchase UI, victory and defeat screens
  • Star rating and coin reward logic
  • Scene lighting, collision sound effects and object-tailored particle effects

10 items · required for submission

Bonus features

  • Collapse replay camera
  • Additional alternative household scene layouts

Attempted only after all mandatory systems are complete

Out of scope

  • High-precision mesh fragmentation
  • NPC animation
  • Weapon numerical upgrade system
  • In-level purchase of cosmetic skins — skins carry no combat bonus

Excluded to protect the solo development schedule

06Prototype progress

What the build already does

A short capture from 11 November 2026, recorded in the iPhone 12 Pro Max simulator, is the first evidence that the core mechanics work together rather than in isolation: a stack that falls, a turret that aims and rotates, and shots that register where they land.

Working in the build

  • Physics collapse. A stack of hard-cover books holds its shape on the coffee table, then falls once the support underneath it is knocked out. Removing one piece moves the rest, instead of everything dropping at the moment of impact.
  • Turret aim, fire and rotation. The reticle follows the aim, the shot leaves the turret, and the turret can be rotated around the table while the camera stays at the fixed overhead angle.
  • No tunnelling at speed. Fast projectiles used to pass straight through meshes between physics steps. Impact is now registered on contact, so a shot that visibly hits a target is counted as a hit.

Video: 11 seconds, iPhone 12 Pro Max simulator, 11 November 2026. The desktop cursor is part of the screen capture; the build itself has no HUD yet.

Where this leaves Phase 2

The three mechanics above are the ones the Phase 2 milestone names first. Still open in that phase: the basic UI, the two-layer resource system with in-level purchase logic, and gathering the final asset set.
